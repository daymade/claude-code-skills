# Source Quality and Independence Rubric

Evaluate a source against the claim it supports. Do not assign one global score and let it authorize unrelated claims.

## Claim-Fitness Questions

1. **Observation:** Could this source directly observe the fact?
2. **Provenance:** Is the underlying record, study, dataset, filing, or interview identifiable?
3. **Method:** Are collection and transformation methods visible enough to evaluate?
4. **Incentive:** Does the author or sponsor benefit from one conclusion?
5. **Freshness:** Is the evidence current enough for this claim?
6. **Precision:** Does the claim stay within the source's population, period, geography, and measurement precision?
7. **Independence:** Does this source add a new evidence-generating process, or only repeat another source?

## Source Roles

| Role | Typical strength | Common limit |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory filing or government record | Entity, ownership, reported financials, awards, dates | The filer may still be the origin of reported claims |
| Peer-reviewed primary study | Measured effect within its design and population | Bias, indirectness, and imprecision remain possible |
| Company/customer first-party record | What the organization did, said, bought, or measured | Does not independently validate its interpretation |
| Supplier engineering case study | Implementation details and named deployment claims | Marketing selection and incomplete failure reporting |
| Reputable journalism | Corroboration, interviews, event reconstruction | May depend on unnamed or shared upstream sources |
| Analyst or industry report | Synthesis, estimates, comparative framing | Methodology and source lineage may be opaque |
| Community report | Leads, user experience, failure signatures | Identity and representativeness may be weak |

Use weak sources as leads or for claims they can actually support. Do not exclude a dated or authorless record automatically when it is the primary artifact itself; instead record the missing metadata as a limitation.

## Evidence Families

Group publications into one evidence family when they derive from the same:

- press release or company disclosure;
- regulatory filing;
- study, sample, or experiment;
- database or modelled estimate;
- interview or anonymous source;
- customer or supplier case study.

Different domains do not make copied evidence independent. Multiple reports may add context, but confidence rises only when the underlying evidence-generation process differs.

## Conflict Handling

- Preserve conflicting values and definitions until the original records are opened.
- Prefer the source that directly observes the claim and uses the relevant definition.
- Explain why values differ when periods, scopes, currencies, or accounting definitions change.
- Keep the conclusion `unknown` when the conflict cannot be resolved.

## Method Foundations

These public primary/official methods inform this rubric:

- [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 4: Searching for and selecting studies](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-04) — plan searches from eligibility criteria, document the search, balance sensitivity and precision, and treat the study rather than each report as the unit of interest.
- [U.S. GAO, Assessing Data Reliability](https://www.gao.gov/assets/710/706666.pdf) — assess reliability in the context of the intended use and increase scrutiny when data alone carries a finding; look for independent corroboration.
- [SEC EDGAR search guidance](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/how-do-i-use-edgar) — use real-time filings and exact filer identifiers for company disclosure research.
- [USAspending Advanced Search and data disclosures](https://www.usaspending.gov/search) — use procurement award records with their published data-source and quality disclosures.
- [GOV.UK Contracts Finder](https://www.gov.uk/contracts-finder) — search public opportunities, tender details, and awarded contracts as buyer-side procurement evidence.
