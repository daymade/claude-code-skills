# Present a version comparison readers can inspect

Use this recipe when an HTML report explains changes between editions of a manual,
proposal or other document. Use the ordinary report gates and review budget. Keep
semantic source verification upstream; arranging two quotes does not establish
novelty, causation or improved real-world results.

## Put interpretation, paired evidence and full text in reading order

1. State the supported change in a complete sentence. Include retained behavior,
   replacements and unresolved contradictions where they limit the conclusion.
   An optional old/new map locates the argument; equal arrows express correspondence,
   not improvement magnitude. Do not substitute document length or question counts.
2. Let readers expand the decisive old and new passages beside that judgment.
   Keep source edition, locator and applicable conditions visible in each passage.
   Include enough surrounding text to distinguish a retained principle from the
   new explanation. Label source assertions separately from verified results.
3. Provide the complete corresponding text for checking omissions and continuity.
   Keep stable section identities, original wording and original-file access.
   Search and text differences help inspection; they are not another quality score.

State a local absence precisely: “not in this corresponding section” does not
mean “absent from the old edition.” Do not render an empty old quote as proof of
that wider claim. Let the source-analysis method decide the searched scope.

Preserve old/new identities when columns stack or shared headers leave view.
Repeat the edition in the quote header; retain explicit column labels on declared
reading widths. Keep unequal passage lengths readable rather than encoding them
as bars or shrinking the longer quotation to create visual symmetry.

## Synthetic reading example

For a fictional support manual, suppose the old troubleshooting chapter already
offers log export, while the new intake chapter repeats log export and adds a
ticket receipt. Present “existing log export is reused during intake; the receipt
step is added.” Put the old troubleshooting passage and new intake passage at that
judgment, then expose the complete chapters. Do not title the comparison “the team
discovers log-based support,” and do not infer that customers received receipts.
This invented example illustrates reading structure; it is not an approved client
template or evidence of a deployed workflow.

For a source chart, retain its exact image and explain which visible relationships
support the adjacent claim. Separate a legible mechanism from any numeric result
the image cannot establish. Preserve unknown units, conditions and missing curves;
do not redraw an incomplete source into apparently complete evidence.

## Verify the actual inspection route

Read an argument, open its paired evidence, identify both editions and conditions,
then inspect the complete section and return. Exercise search, difference display,
deep links and reset only when those controls exist. Include the long-detail
filtering case in [interactive analysis](interactive-analysis.md#exercise-filtered-evidence-and-every-record-selection-entry).
Observe the visible passage and reading position, not just matching DOM nodes.
Keep unresolved source facts explicit; a readable page does not certify them.
