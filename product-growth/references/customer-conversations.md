# Use customer conversations as growth evidence

Load this recipe when supplied sales or support conversations affect the growth
decision. Use the existing source reader and project records; this recipe neither
transcribes audio nor authorizes contacting customers. Keep customer identities,
verbatim transcripts, sector-specific categories and commercial terms private.

## Reconstruct the conversation before grading the response

Bind each call to its source, time anchors, observed customer background and
selection method. Keep uncertain transcription or speaker attribution explicit.
Link earlier promises and later conversations only when the same customer is
established; similar wording or a shared speaker number is insufficient.

Preserve two complementary views when analyzing a collection:

- **Per customer/call:** give the evidenced background, then every customer
  question or stated concern in order, with verbatim wording and source anchors.
- **Across calls:** group those same quotations by meaning or the decision the
  customer is trying to make. Keep stable links to the per-call entries; retain
  the user's supplied category definitions without adding inferred motives.

Treat the journey as potentially recurring decision tasks, not a compulsory
linear funnel. A customer can revisit trust, suitability, value and next-step
arrangements. Separate an observed question from an analyst's interpretation.

Pair each quotation with the staff response, the customer's subsequent reaction
and any later answer. Distinguish an immediate answer gap from a later supplement;
neither erase the initial gap nor label a question unanswered after a relevant
answer appears. Keep the question and follow-up locators inspectable together.

## Identify the missing work and the accepted next step

Check existing answer materials and their current applicability before proposing
new scripts. Distinguish these causes with evidence, retaining unknown when needed:

| Cause | Evidence to look for | Candidate change |
|---|---|---|
| Missing answer | No applicable answer found in the inspected materials | Obtain the missing answer through its existing owner |
| Available answer not used | A current applicable answer exists; the call omits it | Practice recognizing and using that answer |
| Insufficient evidence | A claim is offered without the conditions or proof this decision needs | Supply the relevant verified material or scoped assessment |
| Incomplete handoff | The customer needs another role, but the next contact or task is not established | Repair the existing handoff and check what happened next |

Record exactly what the customer accepts: receiving instructions, another call,
a proposed appointment or a confirmed arrangement. Keep willingness, confirmation,
attendance, successful use and purchase separate. A promise to send material is
not evidence of delivery, understanding or agreement with its contents. Preserve
the project's agreed qualification/counting definitions; do not create a new
billing milestone from an analysis category.

Use the existing action card to connect the barrier to a specific answer,
material or handoff, an authorized owner and the observable next result. Revisit
the same customer's subsequent evidence where available. Keep implementation,
task progress and population growth effects separate. Compare local teams using
their own contexts and compatible definitions; selected success/failure examples
discover mechanisms but do not supply a conversion-rate denominator.

The analyst applies these distinctions; no bundled validator decides whether a
reply resolved the customer's concern. When a review is required, inspect the
paired original passages and any missing follow-up, not just category counts.

## Check the acceptance object with a synthetic example

Treat this invented exchange as a reasoning control, not a customer record:

- Customer, `00:10`: “Can I export the weekly summary without the attachments?”
- Staff, `00:15`: “How many reports do you create?”
- Staff, `01:20`: “The summary export excludes attachments.”
- Customer, `01:30`: “Send the steps; I will check it tomorrow.”

Report an initial answer gap with a later answer at `01:20`. Record acceptance of
receiving instructions, with delivery and successful export still unobserved.
Check the applicable product/version and existing instructions before classifying
the answer's adequacy. Do not infer purchase, a completed task or growth uplift.
