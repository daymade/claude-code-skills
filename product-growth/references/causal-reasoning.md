# Reason from observations to interventions

## Define the effect and alternatives

Name intervention, target population, comparison, outcome, horizon and unit.
Draw a small causal diagram or write the path when it clarifies selection,
confounders, mediators or interference. Separate attributed from incremental
conversions caused by the intervention.

Build competing explanations: product value, distribution, audience mix, external
demand/timing, incentives, automation and measurement. Record support, counterevidence,
reverse causation, unknowns and a discriminating probe. Do not assign causal
percentages that the design cannot identify.

| Evidence | Infer | Check next |
|---|---|---|
| Launch/traffic peak same day | Coincidence and possible mechanism | Exposure before outcome, independent distribution records, comparison |
| Retained users perform action A | Activation candidate | Motivation/opportunity selection and intervention on A |
| Onboarding failure reports | Failure reported for those cases | Reproduce environment/version; estimate exposure separately |
| Platform catalog contains product | Availability at place/date | Default enablement, execution, accepted work separately |
| Installations/copied code | Observed distribution/reuse | Independent task results, versions and projects; keep people unknown |
| Before/after gain | Observed change | Instrumentation, seasonality, mix and a credible comparison |

Treat stated reasons as perspectives. Treat competitor features and editorial
praise as hypotheses, not proof of local demand. Do not read a failure to reject
an effect as proof of no effect.

## Choose the strongest feasible design

For randomization, predefine eligibility, assignment/analysis unit, primary outcome,
meaningful minimum effect, guardrails, exposure/follow-up window, power/detectability
and decision rule. Check allocation/sample-ratio mismatch, logging, contamination,
missingness and multiple comparisons before interpreting results. Analyze assigned
units for intention-to-treat; label exposed-only/exploratory analyses separately.
Avoid stopping at the first favorable conventional p-value; use the planned fixed
horizon or valid sequential method.

Report absolute and relative effects, denominators, uncertainty and practical cost.
State what the interval supports and cannot exclude. A significant gain may cost
more than its value; an inconclusive guardrail may still permit harm. Do not decide
from a p-value alone.

Check spillovers across organizations, sellers, buyers, inventory, social links
and markets. Consider justified cluster, geographic, switching-time or other designs
with a specialist; inspect carryover and power. Do not prescribe a universal cure.

Without randomization, choose a suitable observational design such as matched
comparisons, difference-in-differences or interrupted time series. Inspect overlap,
timing, parallel trends where required, concurrent changes and sensitivity to
unmeasured confounding. State identification assumptions and their possible violations.
Regression controls and model complexity alone do not establish causality.

For small samples, use reproducible task observation, support follow-up or bounded
adoption/payment tests to reduce a named uncertainty. Separate mechanism/usability
evidence from population treatment effects. Do not invent sample sizes, thresholds
or industry benchmarks.

Use the strongest supported conclusion. Report scoped effects from credible
experiments instead of universal doubt. Keep observational mechanisms uncertain;
keep sensitivity scenarios labeled as assumption-driven calculations, not realized
or forecast revenue.
